Delegating Tasks Clearly

Delegando tarefas com clareza

Tempo estimado: 60–90 minutos, divididos ao longo da semana.

Delegar mal em inglês costuma soar vago ("can you take care of this?") ou autoritário demais. Este tema ensina a linguagem exata para delegar com clareza, sem ambiguidade sobre quem é dono do quê.

"Delegating Isn't Just Handing Off Work"

A lot of failed delegation isn't about the task itself — it's about vague language. "Can you take care of this?" leaves ownership, deadline, and expectations completely open. Clear delegation in English usually names three things explicitly: what "done" looks like, who owns it, and when you'll check in.

"I want you to take the reins on the client report. Full ownership — I just need it by Thursday, and I'll check in on Tuesday to see where things stand." This sentence removes almost all ambiguity: outcome, owner, timeline, and a checkpoint, without a single vague verb.

The other half of delegating well is resisting the urge to micromanage after handing something off. Checking in on Tuesday, as promised, is different from asking for updates every day — the first builds trust, the second erodes it.

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à Conversação

Delegar em inglês frequentemente usa a estrutura causativa ("have/get someone to do something") e a voz passiva para atribuir tarefas.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)
Causativo
(have/get + objeto + infinitivo)		I got the design team to review it before launch.Usado para descrever que você fez alguém realizar uma tarefa — comum ao falar de delegação.
Voz passiva
(para atribuir tarefas)		The report will be handled by Sarah this week.Usado para focar na tarefa e em quem é responsável, de forma organizada e neutra.
should have
(expectativa)		You should have full ownership of this by Friday.Usado para comunicar uma expectativa clara sobre prazo e responsabilidade.

Dica prática: na estrutura causativa com "get", use "to" antes do verbo ("get someone TO do"); com "have", não use "to" ("have someone DO"). Os dois têm o mesmo sentido, mas gramática diferente.

ETAPA 6Speaking — Perguntas Abertas

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só — use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. Describe a task you recently delegated. How did you make expectations clear?
  2. What's your process for deciding what to delegate versus what to keep?
  3. How do you check in on a delegated task without micromanaging?
  4. What's a time delegation went wrong, and what did you learn from it?
  5. How do you build enough trust with someone to fully hand off ownership?
  6. What does "full ownership" actually mean to you when you delegate something?
  7. How do you handle it when someone you delegated to doesn't follow through?
  8. What's the hardest part of delegating for you personally?
  9. How has your ability to delegate changed as your bandwidth has changed?
  10. What advice would you give a new manager who struggles to let go of tasks?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "I want you to have full ownership of..." — para deixar clara a responsabilidade.
  • "What does done look like?" — pergunta útil para quem recebe a tarefa.
  • "I'll check in on [dia], but it's yours." — para combinar acompanhamento sem microgerenciar.
  • "I trust you to run with this." — para transmitir confiança ao delegar.
