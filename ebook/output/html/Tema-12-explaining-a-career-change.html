Explaining a Career Change

Explicando uma mudança de carreira

Tempo estimado: 60–90 minutos, divididos ao longo da semana.

Explicar uma mudança de carreira em inglês exige mais do que vocabulário — exige uma narrativa de causa e efeito que faça a mudança parecer uma decisão, não um acidente.

"The Difference Between an Excuse and a Reason"

When people explain a career change badly, it sounds like an excuse: "I just wasn't happy anymore." When they explain it well, it sounds like a decision, built on cause and effect: "I was burning out, and I realized the parts of my job I actually enjoyed had nothing to do with my title — so I made a calculated risk and pivoted into a field that matched that."

The structure that makes a career change sound intentional, in English, usually has three parts: what wasn't working, what you noticed about yourself, and the specific decision that followed. Skipping the middle part — the self-awareness — is what makes a career change sound impulsive instead of considered.

It also helps to name your transferable skills explicitly. Employers and curious listeners alike want to know you didn't start completely from scratch — you carried something valuable with you across the jump.

Roteiro do áudio (Audio Script)

"That's a big shift — from finance to teaching. What made you take the leap?"

"Honestly, I was burning out, and I noticed the part of my job I actually loved was mentoring junior analysts. So it was a calculated risk, but I pivoted into teaching, and a lot of my transferable skills — explaining complex things simply — carried right over."

Perceba a estrutura: o que não estava funcionando → o que a pessoa percebeu sobre si mesma → a decisão específica que seguiu.

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à Conversação

Explicar decisões de carreira depende de conectar causa, propósito e concessão com clareza. Essas três estruturas cobrem isso.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)
because
(motivo)		I left my job because I wanted more creative freedom.Usado para dar o motivo direto de uma decisão.
in order to / so that
(propósito)		I took a pay cut in order to grow faster in a new field.Usado para explicar o objetivo por trás de uma decisão, não só a causa.
despite / in spite of
(concessão)		Despite the risk, I decided to make the jump.Usado para reconhecer um obstáculo antes de afirmar a decisão — soa mais maduro que simplesmente ignorar o risco.

Dica prática: depois de "despite" e "in spite of", use substantivo ou gerúndio ("despite the risk", "despite knowing the risk") — nunca uma frase completa com sujeito e verbo conjugado.

ETAPA 6Speaking — Perguntas Abertas

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só — use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. Have you ever made a career change? Walk me through the decision.
  2. What transferable skills do you think would carry over if you changed careers today?
  3. What's a calculated risk you took professionally that paid off?
  4. How do you know the difference between burning out and just having a hard week?
  5. What would it take for you to leave your comfort zone and start over in a new field?
  6. How do you explain an unconventional career path to someone who's skeptical of it?
  7. What's a skill you didn't expect to be transferable, but was?
  8. If you reinvented yourself professionally tomorrow, what would you pivot into?
  9. What's the biggest fear people have about making a career jump, in your opinion?
  10. Who do you know who took a big career leap? What happened?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "That's what pushed me to..." — para conectar uma dificuldade a uma decisão.
  • "It wasn't an easy decision, but..." — para reconhecer o peso da mudança.
  • "A lot of that carried over, actually." — para falar de habilidades transferíveis.
  • "In hindsight, it was the right call." — para validar a decisão olhando para trás.
