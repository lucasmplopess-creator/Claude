Negotiating Deadlines and Scope

Negociando prazos e escopo

Tempo estimado: 60–90 minutos, divididos ao longo da semana.

"Deadline or Scope, Rarely Both"

One of the most useful ideas in negotiating deadlines and scope, in any language, is that they usually trade off against each other. Fixed deadline, flexible scope, or fixed scope, flexible deadline. Trying to lock both rarely ends well, and English negotiators tend to name this trade-off explicitly.

"If the deadline stays fixed, we'll need to cut scope, here's what I'd prioritize keeping." This kind of sentence turns a difficult negotiation into a structured choice, rather than an argument about whether the deadline is "realistic."

Building in a buffer, and saying so out loud, also helps: "We've built in a small buffer for this deadline, but scope creep would eat into it fast." Naming the risk early makes it much easier to renegotiate later without it feeling like a surprise.

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à ConversaçãoGrammar for Conversation

Negociar prazos exige estruturas de condição e futuro que deixam claro o que depende do quê.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)
would need to
(condição para prazo)		We would need an extra week to cover the new scope.Usado para declarar uma necessidade condicionada a uma mudança de escopo/prazo.
as long as
(condição)		We can hit the deadline, as long as scope doesn't change.Usado para estabelecer uma condição clara para cumprir um compromisso.
by + prazo
(Future / Future Perfect)		We will have the first deliverable ready by Friday.Usado para comunicar um compromisso de prazo de forma precisa.

Dica prática: "as long as" é uma das estruturas mais úteis em negociação de prazos, deixa claro que seu compromisso depende de uma condição específica, sem soar como desculpa.

ETAPA 6Speaking: Perguntas AbertasOpen-Ended Speaking

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só: use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. Describe a recent negotiation you had over a deadline or project scope.
  2. How do you decide what to prioritize when a deadline can't move?
  3. What's your strategy for building in a buffer without it looking like padding?
  4. How do you address scope creep before it derails a deadline?
  5. Describe a time renegotiating a deadline actually improved the outcome.
  6. How do you communicate a delay to stakeholders without losing their trust?
  7. What's the difference between a realistic timeline and an optimistic one, in your experience?
  8. How flexible are you usually willing to be on scope versus deadline?
  9. What's a trade-off between speed and quality you've had to navigate?
  10. How do you lock in a deadline in a way that prevents scope creep later?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "If the deadline stays fixed, we'd need to...", para nomear o trade-off.
  • "Let's lock in the deadline before adding anything else.", para prevenir scope creep.
  • "We've built in a small buffer for this.", para comunicar margem de segurança.
  • "That's a classic case of scope creep.", para nomear o problema com clareza.
