Tempo estimado: 60–90 minutos, divididos ao longo da semana.

"Your Introduction Isn't Your Résumé"

At a networking event, the instinct is to introduce yourself the way you would in a job interview: full title, full history. In English, this usually backfires, it's too much information for a first exchange, and it leaves no room for the other person to react or ask something.

A better self-introduction is short and leaves a hook: "I'm Marina, I work in product design, mostly for fintech startups." That's it. It's specific enough to be interesting, short enough to invite a question back, "Oh, which ones?", and it doesn't try to say everything at once.

The other half of a good introduction is purpose: saying briefly why you're there. "I'm here to meet people working on AI in healthcare" gives the other person something concrete to respond to, far more useful than a generic "just checking things out."

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à ConversaçãoGrammar for Conversation

Se apresentar profissionalmente em inglês depende de afirmar sua identidade no presente e declarar seu propósito com clareza.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)
Present Simple
(identidade profissional)		I work in product design, mostly for SaaS companies.Usado para descrever o que você faz de forma estável, sua profissão, seu foco.
I'm here to / I'm here because
(propósito)		I'm here to meet people in the fintech space.Usado para declarar o motivo de estar em um evento, dá ao outro algo concreto para responder.
Question tag
(convite à resposta)		You're speaking on the panel later, aren't you?Usado para convidar a outra pessoa a confirmar ou completar uma informação, mantendo a conversa fluindo.

Dica prática: question tags invertem a polaridade da frase (afirmativa → negativa e vice-versa): "You're with Google, aren't you?" / "You're not from here, are you?"

ETAPA 6Speaking: Perguntas AbertasOpen-Ended Speaking

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só: use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. How do you usually introduce yourself at a networking event?
  2. What's your go-to icebreaker when approaching someone you don't know?
  3. How do you decide who to approach first at a crowded event?
  4. What's a self-introduction you've heard that really stuck with you? Why?
  5. How do you handle it when your introduction doesn't get much of a reaction?
  6. What's the difference between a confident introduction and a rehearsed-sounding one?
  7. How does your self-introduction change depending on the type of event?
  8. What's something you always try to include in your elevator pitch?
  9. How do you make sure you're remembered after a short introduction?
  10. What's the worst networking introduction you've ever given or witnessed?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "I don't think we've met, I'm...", abertura clássica e segura.
  • "What brings you here?", icebreaker universal em eventos.
  • "Small world!", para reagir a uma conexão inesperada.
  • "I'd love to hear more about that.", para convidar a pessoa a continuar falando.
