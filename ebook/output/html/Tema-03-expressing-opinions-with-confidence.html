Expressando opiniões com confiança

Tempo estimado: 60–90 minutos, divididos ao longo da semana.

"Confidence Isn't About Being Right"

One of the biggest misconceptions about expressing opinions in a second language is that confidence comes from certainty. It doesn't. Confidence comes from structure, from knowing how to open an opinion, how to soften it if needed, and how to back it up, regardless of whether you're 100% sure.

Native speakers hedge their opinions constantly: "I could be wrong, but...", "My take is...", "Arguably...". These aren't signs of weakness, they're tools that let you commit to a position without pretending to have all the answers. Using them well actually makes you sound more credible, not less.

The real skill isn't having a strong opinion on everything. It's being able to say "I think X, because Y" clearly, and being just as comfortable saying "I'm not fully convinced" when that's the honest answer.

Roteiro do áudio (Audio Script)

"What's your take on the new remote work policy?"

"Honestly? I'd argue it's a step in the right direction, though I could be wrong. From my standpoint, productivity hasn't dropped at all, arguably it's even improved. But I get that not everyone sees eye to eye on this."

Note como a pessoa usa "I'd argue", "from my standpoint" e "arguably" na mesma resposta, três formas diferentes de reforçar a mesma opinião.

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à ConversaçãoGrammar for Conversation

A força de uma opinião em inglês muda conforme a estrutura que você escolhe, não só o vocabulário. Veja os três níveis abaixo.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)
Opinião leve
(I think / I feel like)		I think we should wait a bit longer.Usado para opiniões mais leves, menos definitivas, soa aberto a mudar de ideia.
Opinião forte
(I'd argue that / I'm convinced that)		I'd argue that remote work actually increases productivity.Usado para posições fortes, quando você quer soar mais seguro e direto.
Opinião suavizada
(It seems to me that / My take is)		It seems to me that we're overcomplicating this.Usado para suavizar uma opinião forte sem parecer inseguro, muito comum em reuniões.

Dica prática: comece opiniões difíceis com "I could be wrong, but...", isso não te enfraquece, te dá espaço para discordar sem soar arrogante.

ETAPA 6Speaking: Perguntas AbertasOpen-Ended Speaking

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só: use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. What's an opinion you hold that most people around you disagree with?
  2. How do you usually back up your opinions when someone pushes back?
  3. Is there a topic where your opinion has changed a lot over the years? What changed it?
  4. How comfortable are you playing devil's advocate in a group discussion?
  5. What's the difference between having a strong opinion and being stubborn, in your view?
  6. Do you adjust how you express opinions depending on who you're talking to? How?
  7. What's an opinion about work or business that you feel strongly about?
  8. How do you know when it's worth pushing your point versus letting it go?
  9. What's a gut feeling you had that turned out to be right (or wrong)?
  10. If you had to convince a skeptical audience of one idea right now, what would it be?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "From where I stand...", para introduzir seu ponto de vista.
  • "I could be wrong, but...", para suavizar uma opinião forte.
  • "Correct me if I'm off here, but...", para abrir espaço para ser contestado.
  • "That's a fair point, but...", para reconhecer o outro lado antes de discordar.
