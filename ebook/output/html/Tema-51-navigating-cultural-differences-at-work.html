"Cultural Generalizations Are Useful — Carefully"

Talking about cultural differences in English requires a specific kind of care: generalizations can be useful ("this culture tends to favor indirect feedback") but need to be held loosely, as tendencies, not rules about individuals.

The safest language hedges appropriately: "tend to," "in my experience," "generally speaking" — all signal that you're describing a pattern, not a fixed truth about every person from that background. "It's not that they're being evasive, it's that direct criticism just isn't the norm there" reframes a misunderstanding as cultural, not personal.

It also helps to name your own missteps honestly: "I misread the silence as agreement — that was a faux pas on my part, not theirs." Owning your own cultural blind spot, rather than blaming the other culture for confusion, tends to build more trust on cross-cultural teams.

Roteiro do áudio (Audio Script)

"I noticed the team went quiet when I asked for direct feedback on the proposal."

"That tracks, actually — in my experience, this team tends to give feedback more indirectly. It's not that they disagree, it's that direct criticism in a group setting isn't really the norm here."

Note o uso de "tend to" e "it's not that X, it's that Y" — generalizações cuidadosas, não afirmações absolutas.

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à Conversação

Fazer generalizações culturais com responsabilidade em inglês depende de verbos de tendência e de estruturas de contraste refinado.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)

(generalização cuidadosa)		This culture tends to favor indirect feedback.Usado para descrever um padrão, não uma regra absoluta sobre todos os indivíduos.

Dica prática: sempre que fizer uma generalização cultural, use "tend to" ou "in my experience" — isso marca a frase como observação, não como regra fixa sobre um grupo inteiro de pessoas.

ETAPA 6Speaking — Perguntas Abertas

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só — use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. Describe a cultural misunderstanding you've experienced or witnessed at work.
  2. What's a cultural norm around communication you've had to adapt to?
  3. How do you check your assumptions before reacting to an unfamiliar situation?
  4. What's the difference between a helpful cultural generalization and a stereotype?
  5. How does hierarchy show up differently across the cultures you've worked with?
  6. What's a faux pas you've made or nearly made while working across cultures?
  7. How do you build cultural competence over time, in your experience?
  8. What's harder for you: adapting to more direct cultures or more indirect ones?
  9. How do you handle a situation where you misread someone's silence or tone?
  10. What advice would you give someone about to join a highly multicultural team?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "In my experience, this culture tends to..." — generalização cuidadosa.
  • "It's not that X, it's that Y." — para reformular um mal-entendido.
  • "That was a faux pas on my part." — para assumir um erro cultural com humildade.
  • "I try to check my assumptions first." — para mostrar abertura cultural.
