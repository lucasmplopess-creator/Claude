Talking About Strengths and Weaknesses

Falando sobre pontos fortes e fracos

Tempo estimado: 60–90 minutos, divididos ao longo da semana.

Falar sobre pontos fortes sem soar arrogante, e sobre fraquezas sem soar despreparado, é um equilíbrio que trava até falantes fluentes. Este tema te dá a estrutura para isso.

"The Honest Way to Talk About Weaknesses"

The classic trap when talking about weaknesses is the fake one: "I work too hard" or "I'm a perfectionist." Interviewers and colleagues see through this instantly, and it usually backfires — it signals you're not self-aware enough to name a real weakness.

A better approach names something genuine, paired with what you're doing about it: "I used to struggle with delegating — I wanted to control every detail. I've been working on it by setting clear check-ins instead of micromanaging." This is honest, specific, and shows growth instead of just confession.

The same logic applies to strengths: instead of a generic "I'm a hard worker," name something specific and back it with a moment: "I excel at staying calm under pressure — during our biggest outage last year, I was the one who kept the team focused." Specificity is what makes both strengths and weaknesses sound credible.

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à Conversação

Falar sobre pontos fortes e fracos depende de verbos e estruturas específicas em inglês. Domine essas três.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)
to excel at / to be good at
+ gerúndio		I excel at managing multiple projects at once.Usado para descrever pontos fortes — sempre seguido de verbo com -ing, não infinitivo.
to struggle with
+ gerúndio		I struggle with public speaking, if I'm honest.Usado para descrever dificuldades de forma direta, mas sem soar dramático.
Comparativos para autoavaliaçãoI'm much better at writing than speaking spontaneously.Usado para comparar dois pontos fortes/fracos entre si, dando contexto relativo.

Dica prática: depois de "excel at", "struggle with" e "good at", o verbo sempre vai para a forma -ing (gerúndio) — nunca infinitivo ("to + verbo").

ETAPA 6Speaking — Perguntas Abertas

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só — use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. What would you say is your single biggest professional strength?
  2. What's a weakness you've genuinely worked to improve — and how?
  3. How do you leverage your strengths day to day in your current job?
  4. What's a blind spot someone pointed out to you that you hadn't noticed?
  5. How do you usually respond when someone challenges you on a weakness in an interview?
  6. What's an area of growth you're actively focused on this year?
  7. Do you think it's ever okay to hide a weakness professionally? Why or why not?
  8. How does your team play to each other's strengths?
  9. What's something you used to struggle with, but have gotten much better at?
  10. How self-aware do you think you are about your own weaknesses? What tells you that?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "If I'm being honest with myself..." — para introduzir uma fraqueza real.
  • "I've been actively working on..." — para mostrar progresso sobre um ponto fraco.
  • "That's definitely one of my strong suits." — para afirmar um ponto forte com confiança.
  • "It's still a work in progress, but..." — para suavizar uma fraqueza sem minimizá-la.
