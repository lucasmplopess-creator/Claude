Talking About Cultural Etiquette Abroad

Falando sobre etiqueta cultural no exterior

Tempo estimado: 60–90 minutos, divididos ao longo da semana.

"The Same Gesture Doesn't Travel the Same Way"

A gesture, dress code, or level of punctuality that's completely normal at home can carry a very different meaning abroad — and English gives you specific, formal structures for naming these customs respectfully: "it's customary to...," "you're expected to...," "it's considered rude to..."

"It's customary to exchange business cards with both hands here, and it's considered rude to write on someone's card in front of them." This kind of sentence names a specific custom clearly, without judgment, in a way locals and travelers both find genuinely useful.

Researching beforehand and asking directly when unsure both signal respect: "I want to make sure I don't make a cultural faux pas — is there anything specific I should know before the meeting?" is a welcomed question in almost any professional context, anywhere.

ETAPA 5Gramática de Apoio Aplicada à Conversação

Falar sobre etiqueta cultural depende de estruturas formais fixas para costume e obrigação social.

EstruturaExemplo (EN)Tradução (PT)
It's customary to...It's customary to remove your shoes before entering.Estrutura formal para descrever um costume comum em determinada cultura.
You're expected to...You're expected to arrive a few minutes early.Usado para descrever uma expectativa social ou profissional específica.
It's considered rude to...It's considered rude to write on someone's business card.Usado para nomear um comportamento visto como falta de educação em um contexto específico.

Dica prática: essas três estruturas ("it's customary to", "you're expected to", "it's considered rude to") são praticamente intercambiáveis em formalidade — escolha com base no que você quer enfatizar: costume, expectativa ou ofensa.

ETAPA 6Speaking — Perguntas Abertas

Responda em voz alta, gravando se possível. Não vale responder em uma frase só — use o que você escreveu na Etapa 4 como base, mas fale livremente.

  1. Describe a cultural etiquette norm you've had to learn while traveling or working abroad.
  2. What's a gesture or custom that means something different in another culture you're aware of?
  3. How would you ask a colleague to brief you on local etiquette before an important meeting?
  4. What's a cultural faux pas you nearly made, or did make, while traveling?
  5. How strict are punctuality norms in the cultures you've worked with, compared to your own?
  6. What's gift-giving etiquette like in a culture you're familiar with?
  7. How do you research cultural etiquette before an important trip?
  8. What's the most surprising etiquette difference you've encountered abroad?
  9. How do you gracefully recover if you make a cultural mistake in front of others?
  10. What etiquette advice would you give someone visiting your country for business the first time?

Expressões úteis para esta conversa:

  • "It's customary to..." — para descrever um costume local.
  • "You're expected to..." — para nomear uma expectativa social.
  • "It's considered rude to..." — para alertar sobre um comportamento ofensivo.
  • "Is there anything I should know, etiquette-wise?" — pergunta preventiva e respeitosa.
